# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Primary (daily active):** College students under GPA pressure who need to build genuine critical thinking skills — not bypass them. They're smart, high-tech-literacy, and rationally tempted to outsource cognition to AI. They need a tool that makes doing the hard thinking feel possible and worthwhile, not punishing.

**Secondary (setup + review):** College instructors and professors who assign analytical and written work. They want visibility into student reasoning — the equivalent of reading rough drafts without asking for them — without becoming AI police.

**Key personas (from PRD):**
- Sofia R. — junior, scholarship student, knows AI is a crutch but uses it anyway to keep up
- Jamie K. — first-gen sophomore, copies AI output because they don't know how to start on their own
- Prof. Diana Ramirez — Economics professor, wants to assess understanding not polished output, frustrated by unreliable AI detection

## Product Purpose

Workbench teaches college students to use AI effectively by requiring structured reflection before AI unlocks. Students respond to scaffolded prompts ("What's your initial take?", "Where are you stuck?") that build their thinking before AI enters the conversation. The AI then builds on what the student has already articulated — not starting from scratch for them.

Instructors see the full process log: original reflection, AI conversation, and final output side by side. Success means instructors can see whether students are thinking, not just whether they produced polished output.

The core bet: structured pre-AI reflection improves both assignment quality and knowledge retention compared to unrestricted AI usage.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, honest, encouraging.

Like a brilliant TA who respects the student's intelligence, tells it straight, and cheers them on. Not a life coach. Not a drill sergeant. Not a cheerful mascot.

Voice is direct and warm — never corporate, never condescending, never artificially hyped.

## References

**Reflect / Obsidian** — thinking tool energy. Note-taking meets structured reflection. Cerebral, intentional, built for people who take their own thinking seriously. Focused without being spartan.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS / Notion-clone** — flat gray palettes, no personality, sidebar + content pane with zero identity. Workbench should feel like a distinct, opinionated product.
- **AI product clichés** — gradient glows on everything, glassy frosted cards, purple-to-blue hero gradients, chatbot bubble UIs, "neural network" visual metaphors. These undermine the tool's core message: AI assists thinking, it doesn't replace it.
- **Corporate LMS (Canvas / Blackboard)** — dense tables, institutional blue, bureaucratic coldness. Workbench is not an enterprise procurement decision; it's a tool students actually want to open.
- **Hyper-gamified EdTech (Duolingo-style)** — streaks, XP bars, mascots, confetti for every action. Some playfulness is right; cartoon energy is not. Students are adults doing serious cognitive work.

## Design Principles

1. **The thinking comes first.** The UI must never make shortcuts easier than doing the work. Every screen should make the path of least resistance the right path.
2. **Process over output.** Expose the journey, not just the destination. Show students their own reflection quality as a signal of their growth.
3. **Calibrated warmth.** Sharp and focused like a precision tool. Warm and encouraging like a mentor who believes in you. Never clinical, never patronizing.
4. **Structured without being institutional.** Organized, legible hierarchies that feel purposeful — not bureaucratic grid layouts inherited from spreadsheets.
5. **Earned delight.** Moments of reward and personality should feel earned by the student's effort. Not sprinkled everywhere to manufacture engagement.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced motion support for gate progress animations and transitions. Color palette must not rely on hue alone to distinguish states (teal/amber roles must also differ in value and/or iconography).
